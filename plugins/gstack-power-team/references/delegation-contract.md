# Delegation Contract

Modification notice: Rewritten and consolidated by Pablo Poo. Attribution and applicable terms: [NOTICE](../NOTICE.md).

Use collaboration when independent contributions materially improve quality or completion time. Keep one lead responsible for requirements, dependencies, integration, and the final result. Simple work can be completed by one person or agent.

- Assign an objective, acceptance criteria, evidence, ownership, and proportionate time or resource limits. Account for concurrent commitments; preserve others' work and avoid overlapping changes.
- Choose collaborators by the judgment and knowledge needed. Existing user role assignments take precedence; capabilities are not fixed intelligence tiers.
- Share the minimum context sufficient for a correct contribution. Preserve requirements and accepted decisions when passing work between phases.
- Acknowledgement is not completion. Obtain the exact result, its evidence, remaining limitations, and next owner action before treating a dependent phase as satisfied. The lead verifies the combined outcome; individual contributions do not establish integrated success.
- Readers evaluating one candidate finish or are confirmed stopped before that candidate changes. The lead consolidates findings before assigning corrections, then refreshes affected review evidence.
- If a contribution fails or ends early, reconcile partial work before continuing. Missing evidence remains missing; a failed or empty review is not a clean result.
- Keep approval authority with the person who holds it. Preserve the source, scope, and conditions of an approval when relaying it; a collaborator's assignment cannot expand scope or authorize action on the user's behalf. Unavailable authority leaves a required decision pending.
- Keep findings, recommendations, accepted limitations, and unresolved questions distinct. Report whether an independent review actually occurred.
- When independent collaboration is unavailable, complete authorized work with the available capabilities and disclose the resulting limits.

The same method applies to a single lead with reviewers or a small agent team. It requires no particular dispatch mechanism.
